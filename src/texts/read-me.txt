Sprite Machine Read Me

Sprite Machine turns pixel-art face sprites into a real, rotatable 3D model. You draw the faces of an object the way a draughtsman draws a plan — the front, a side, the top — and the machine carves a voxel solid that every face agrees on, with the chunky look baked into the geometry.

THE SHEET

A document is one sprite sheet: six square tiles in a 3 × 2 grid.

    LEFT     FRONT    TOP
    RIGHT    BACK     BOTTOM

Every tile is a slice of the same lattice, so a pixel's place in its tile is its place in the object. A FRONT pixel becomes solid only where the side view covers its row and the top view covers its column, which is why the faces must be drawn in register. Empty tiles are fine: a face with no art of its own is filled from its opposite.

DRAWING

Double-click a document to open it, or choose File → New… (⌃N). The Tools palette holds the selection, pencil, rectangle, fill, eraser and eyedropper; the strip under the menu bar holds the live tool's options, and the current ink is the swatch at its left — click it, or choose Edit → Pick Color… (⌘K), for the Colors dialog.

The Color Palette under the Tools palette has a swatch for every color in the document. Press a swatch to paint with its color; the ink's swatch is ringed. Widen the window for more columns.

The Full Sprite View shows all six faces in a row; click a tile, or a cube in its header, to switch the face you are editing. The 3D View rebuilds as you draw. The faded art behind the canvas is the opposite face, and the strips just outside its edges are the neighbouring faces' nearest lines — line your pixels up against them.

Edit → Tile Size… retiles the sheet to any square size from 1 to 64.

LAYERS

A document can hold up to eight layers. Each is a full set of six faces at the document's tile size, stacked below the first in the sheet. Every layer carves its own solid and the model is all of them together, so shapes one set of faces can't carve, like wheels apart from the body above them, can be drawn as separate layers. Where layers overlap, the later layer's colors show.

Layer → New Layer adds a layer and makes it the one you edit. Delete Layer, Rename Layer…, Move Layer Up and Move Layer Down act on the edited layer, and the list at the foot of the menu, the layer popup at the foot of the document window, or the keys 1 to 8, switch layers. To copy a face to another layer, press ⌘A and ⌘C, press that layer's number and press ⌘V: the pixels arrive in the same place, selected, ready to drag. While you edit a layer, the faded art behind the canvas includes the other layers' art on the same face, each over its layer's opposite face, mirrored. The Full Sprite View shows this layer's tiles alone. The strips outside the canvas show this layer's neighbouring faces alone. Check single layer in the 3D View's header to see only the layer you are editing, in its place in the model; the 3D Sprite Atlas and the exports still use every layer.

SAVING AND FILING

Documents are saved in the browser (File → Save, ⌘S) and live as icons on the desktop. Drag them into folders, rename them from their icons, and drag them to the Trash to delete them; Empty Trash… in the Finder's Special menu is what removes them for good. File → Download saves the sheet as a .png, which is the document itself: drop it back onto the desktop to open it again.

EXPORTING

File → Export 3D Model… writes the model as one glTF binary (.glb) with its texture inside, for Unity, Godot, Unreal, three.js or any glTF viewer. File → Export Sprite Atlas… renders the model from a ring of angles into a sprite sheet with a TexturePacker JSON beside it, for a 2D engine; View → 3D Sprite Atlas shows the ring and its settings.
